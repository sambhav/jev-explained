AI that helps software decide
what to do next.

A customer says their checkout is broken. Your product needs to pick a team, assess urgency and decide whether to interrupt someone. Jev is an AI model designed for those small judgments: give it the situation and the possible answers, and it returns a decision with probabilities.

03 / WHY PRODUCT TEAMS SHOULD CARE

Lower decision costs change what you can afford to build.

Today you might review only a sample of agent runs, skip a relevance check, or accept slow routing because another model call is too costly. If a smaller judgment becomes fast and inexpensive at the quality you need, you can put it in more places.

01

Respond while the user is still there

Route a request or select the next useful action inside an interactive experience.

Measure: p95 time to a useful action.
02

Spend judgment on more events

Assess every retrieved candidate or agent step instead of sampling a small fraction.

Measure: coverage at a fixed budget.
03

Make uncertainty part of the workflow

Handle clear cases automatically and reserve attention for ambiguous ones.

Measure: correct automation at a fixed error rate.
04

Keep complex work with the right model

Use a reasoning model for planning and a generative model for writing, with fast judgments between those steps.

Measure: cost per successfully completed task.

The potential step change is affordable judgment throughout a system: before an action, after a tool result, and across all the candidates the user never sees.

That is a product hypothesis, not a universal speedup. The benefits depend on accuracy, network delay, fallback frequency and how much of the task actually consists of bounded decisions. Change those assumptions in the lab →

Three boundaries worth remembering

A valid answer can be wrong

Selecting a real team avoids an invented team name. It does not prove the selected team is correct.

A probability needs validation

Test whether predictions near 80% are correct roughly 80% of the time on your own data.

Some work still needs generation

Writing a reply, creating code and working through a new plan remain natural jobs for generative models.
